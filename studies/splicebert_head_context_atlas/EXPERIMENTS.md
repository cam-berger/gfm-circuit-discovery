# Experimental hypothesis registry — SpliceBERT Head–Context Atlas

> **Tier: T2 — provenance.** Supporting record: preregistrations, decision logs, correction ledgers, audits.

Status: **working roadmap, not a preregistration** (2026-07-19).

This document records probable working hypotheses generated by the audited 96-head/six-MLP screen,
the gene-disjoint whole-component validation, and the earlier carrier studies. It is deliberately
forward-looking. A hypothesis enters execution only after its estimand, controls, power, multiplicity,
stopping rules, and immutable outputs are formalized, red-teamed, and frozen.

The organizing goal remains:

> component × biological context × model behavior × causal mechanism

The conditional Stage 2 mechanism run is complete and independently audited under the outcome-blind A3
numerical amendment. Its qualified mechanism results are incorporated below.

## Boundaries and notation

- `A = margin(native) − margin(component zeroed)`: component necessity for native masked-site
  reconstruction.
- `B = margin(native) − margin(component patched from the partner-destroyed state)`: component
  mediation of donor–acceptor partner dependence.
- The practical effect floor is `tau = 0.05 nats` unless a future preregistration independently
  justifies another value.
- A and B are different causal interventions. Their apparent component-class separation must be tested
  with matched interventions before it is called an architectural division of labor.
- SpliceBERT is an MLM, not a splice-usage predictor. “Steering site choice” initially means steering a
  **model-native relative partner-coupling/readout estimand**, not changing cellular splice usage.
- Probes, SVD axes, attention, gradients, and attribution DAGs generate candidate directions. Only
  interventions can establish necessity, sufficiency, or mediation.

## Evidence snapshot motivating the roadmap

1. Twenty-four cross-role general profiles passed the discovery eligibility gates. The frozen cap
   advanced four: `L2H15/A`, `L3H1/A`, `L1H12/A`, and `L2MLP/B`. All four whole-component relations
   reproduced for donors and acceptors on gene-disjoint internal validation.
2. The eligible A family contained 17 attention heads. The eligible B family contained all six MLPs
   and only one attention head, `L3H1`.
3. All 20 material, direction-stable context signals occurred in only three contrasts: acceptor
   clusters (11), donor clusters (6), and cassette-exon donors (3). None passed the separate
   `MDE80 <= 0.05` progression gate, so none is a validated context-selectivity result.
4. `L5H6/A` was role-opposed in discovery: it supported acceptor reconstruction but suppressed donor
   reconstruction, without a broad masked-MLM health flag.
5. The carrier program independently places a distributed period-3 routing coordinate in `L3H1`'s
   attention pattern rather than its value payload.
6. Stage 2 identifies controlled downstream MLP recovery for every validated A-head: L2H15 at L2–L5 in
   both roles; L3H1 at donor L3–L5 and acceptor L3/L5; L1H12 at donor L1–L5 and acceptor L2/L3/L5.
   L2MLP/B remains upstream-path instrument-limited because no attention head in the frozen B shortlist
   validated; this is missing instrumentation, not evidence that no upstream route exists.

## Causal site-choice steering program

### Scientific question

For two same-type candidate sites `i` and `j` sharing a partner `p`, can we identify an internal variable
that distinguishes which site SpliceBERT couples more strongly to `p`, then causally transfer that
variable from the preferred site to the alternative site?

This is more stringent than increasing the masked nucleotide margin at `j`. A successful intervention
must change **relative, partner-specific behavior** within the choice set.

### Primary model-native estimand

For candidate site `i` and shared partner `p`, define:

`B_i(p) = margin_i(native) − margin_i(p destroyed)`.

For a two-site choice set, define relative coupling:

`R_ij(p) = B_i(p) − B_j(p)`.

For larger choice sets, report the centered vector `B_i(p) − mean_k B_k(p)` and the rank/order of sites.
The primary steering outcome is the paired change in `R_ij(p)` after intervention at `j`. A stronger
claim requires a sign reversal—`j` becomes preferred over `i`—but a material monotone shift can support
partial steering.

Absolute masked margins, MaxEnt scores, and attention weights remain diagnostics. They cannot substitute
for `R_ij(p)`.

### S1 — establish that there is a learnable choice representation

1. Construct donor clusters and acceptor clusters with a shared partner and at least two visible,
   coordinate-valid same-type sites.
2. Match or residualize target/partner MaxEnt, distance, phase, CDS/UTR, composition, transcript support,
   absolute packet position, packet geometry, and gene. Absolute MaxEnt is a covariate, not the semantic
   definition of “better.” Require transfer across held-out position and distance strata so a learned axis
   cannot be an absolute-position shortcut.
3. On discovery genes only, estimate candidate layerwise directions from within-choice-set activation
   differences. Candidate methods may include a cross-fitted mean-difference axis, regularized linear
   discriminant, or low-rank SVD/PLS subspace.
4. Compare the learned axis with existing `U_info`, `U_read`, site-identity, unembedding/logit, and
   MaxEnt-associated directions. Similarity is descriptive; distinct causal interventions decide their
   roles.
5. Correct across the full layer/component/direction scan. Freeze one direction or the smallest stable
   subspace before accessing validation genes.

Representation gate: held-out prediction of `R_ij(p)` must exceed shuffled-within-gene labels and matched
sequence-only baselines across independently seeded gene draws. Passing this gate licenses intervention,
not a mechanism claim.

### S2 — activation steering sufficiency

At frozen layer `l`, intervene at the less-preferred site `j`:

`h_l(j) <- h_l(j) + alpha * u_choice`, for a prespecified symmetric dose grid.

Because the P3 route is distributed, compare three predeclared loci rather than assuming a single token:

1. the candidate-site residual position;
2. the `L3H1` attention pattern over the candidate–partner span; and
3. the candidate-position `L2MLP` write or the Stage 2-licensed downstream write.

Required controls:

- negative dose and sign reversal;
- equal-norm random orthogonal directions with max-statistic correction;
- equal-variance principal-component controls;
- `U_read`, `U_info`, unembedding, and site-identity direction controls;
- injection at a distance/phase-matched non-site position;
- injection at the currently preferred site;
- decoy or non-partner sequence controls;
- residual-norm/OOD gates and masked non-site MLM-health probes.

Primary support requires a monotone, sign-specific change in `R_ij(p)` exceeding the meaningful floor
and the corrected random-direction family on untouched genes. A raw rise in `margin_j` with no relative
or partner-specific redistribution is insufficient.

### S3 — necessity and reversibility

At naturally preferred sites, remove the frozen choice direction by projection or add its negative.
Then restore the direction. Require loss and recovery of relative partner coupling without broad MLM
damage. This distinguishes a causally used direction from an arbitrary vector that can force the final
readout.

### S4 — partner and context specificity

Repeat the intervention while varying the partner:

- native shared partner;
- distance/strength-matched non-partner;
- destroyed partner;
- donor-cluster versus acceptor-cluster topology;
- single-pair packets without a choice set.

If the direction increases every site margin or works without the partner, name it a site-confidence or
readout direction—not a pairing/choice direction. If it operates only in one topology, retain the
context-qualified label.

### S5 — biological bridge

Only after causal model-native steering is established should “better splice site” be grounded in
external biology. Freeze the direction and test whether its native projection predicts independent
junction usage or PSI from a provenance-pinned RNA-seq/junction resource, controlling the same sequence
covariates. A prospective minigene or MPSA perturbation is required to claim that direction-inducing
sequence edits alter real splice choice.

### Evidence labels

1. **Choice-decodable direction:** predicts held-out `R`, no causal claim.
2. **Model-native steering direction:** activation intervention shifts relative partner coupling.
3. **Circuit-mediated steering variable:** a frozen head/MLP/path intervention blocks and restores the
   effect.
4. **Biologically aligned direction:** predicts independent junction usage after covariate control.
5. **Biologically causal steering program:** constrained sequence edits change measured splice usage.

No level silently inherits the label of the next.

## Recommended execution order

1. **Completed:** the zero-failure, `export_eligible=true` representative bundle is now source-hash-pinned
   in the visualizer with evidence labels, intervals, provenance hashes, and remainder accounting intact.
2. Return to the original atlas intent: perform an outcome-independent cohort/MDE redesign for the
   acceptor-cluster, donor-cluster, and cassette-donor signals, targeting `MDE80 <= 0.05` without relaxing
   balance, specificity, or multiplicity.
3. In parallel at low compute cost, census the site-choice steering cohort and preflight whether relative
   coupling `R_ij(p)` is measurable across position/distance strata.
4. Only after those censuses, freeze either the powered context validation or a small discovery-only
   steering instrument pilot: direction recovery, random controls, dose/OOD,
   determinism, and partner-specific sensitivity. Do not read validation outcomes.
5. Run confirmation only when its behavioral and power
   gates pass. Do not spend chr8/9 merely because a direction is visually compelling.

## Drift guard

For every proposed run, record:

1. Which atlas cell and hypothesis ID it tests.
2. The biological context and model-native behavioral estimand.
3. The intervention and its matched null family.
4. The positive control proving the instrument can see the relevant route and distance.
5. The result that retires the hypothesis.
6. The strongest evidence label the result could license.
7. Why existing artifacts cannot already answer the question.
