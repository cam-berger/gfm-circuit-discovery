Six experiment phases on the same n=200 real 3′ splice-site windows: the founding six-effect pilot, a control-and-composition fix, an absolute-vs-floor test, a per-sequence branch-point relocation, an attention-level head content-control, and a causal intervention ladder. Each phase is a check on the one before it.

Phase 1 — control fix: is it real, and is it order or composition?

1
Control & composition
The far-intronic floor is tiny (+0.046) — real effects clear it easily. But shuffling the tract in place barely dents its effect: this is an order/identity signal, not bulk pyrimidine density.

A — the matched noise floor

Content-destroying corruption of a region with no known functional role, deep in the intron (position 40–55) — the honest replacement for GUE's non-splice-label control, which wasn't reliably intronic.

B — order vs. composition: content-destroying vs. shuffle-in-place

Same region, two corruption methods: full replacement (destroys composition and order) vs. a permutation of the same bases (destroys order, keeps composition exactly).

content-destroying shuffle-in-place

The floor (+0.046) is 5–27× smaller than every real effect, so context, AG-scanning, and the tract effect are unambiguous. The branch-point effect (+0.212) sits right at its edge — the better control didn't clear it, it just made "looks real" honest: marginal.

The bigger surprise is panel B: PPT shuffle-in-place still shows +1.207, almost matching full corruption (+1.245). Reordering the same bases already destroys nearly the whole signal — so whatever SpliceBERT reads in the tract is a specific pattern or position-level dependency, not aggregate pyrimidine content.

Gate vs. artifact

Absolute m_acc by condition, against the random-AG floor

Reuses Phase 0's per-sample values directly (they were already absolute, not differenced) — this is a re-analysis, not a re-run.

clearly above floor indistinguishable from floor

A Δ alone can't tell a real interaction from a corruption method that's already hit bottom. In absolute terms, PPT disruption alone lands statistically on the random-AG floor — the same confidence as no real context at all. BP disruption alone stays well above it, retaining real signal.

That rules out reading "both disrupted" as an AND-gate: PPT alone already explains the collapse. The simpler picture survives — the tract is close to sufficient by itself; the branch point adds a smaller, dispensable increment on top of it.

Phase 3 — real coordinates instead of a fixed offset

results/phase3_results.json
3
Relocate, don't assume
Per-sequence branch-point localization doesn't rescue the branch-point effect — it shrinks further and loses significance, while the tract effect grows.

Fixed-offset (Phase 0) vs. per-sequence located coordinate

Branch point located per sequence by a yUnAy-consensus scan over the canonical 18–45nt upstream window (2/200 sequences had no candidate and fell back to the fixed offset); tract redefined as everything between the located branch point and the AG (mean 27nt vs. the fixed 15nt window).

fixed offset real coordinate

If the fixed 23nt branch-point window was simply mislocated, giving the model its real, per-sequence branch point should have grown the effect. Instead it shrinks (+0.212 → +0.090) and its CI now straddles zero. The tract effect, given more accurate (and longer, on average) boundaries, grows instead (+1.245 → +1.433).

Mislocation is not what was hiding a bigger branch-point signal. The honest reading: no positive evidence of a standalone, legible branch-point effect survives three independent checks — pending a real branchpointer/LaBranchoR annotation, since this project's locator is a documented PWM stand-in, not those tools.

Phase 4 — head content-control, retargeted to tract-readers

results/phase4_results.json
4
Content vs. locality
All 6 donor-circuit candidate heads are saturated positional-locality heads — 0/6 pass. A data-driven ranking of all 96 heads found 5 new heads that genuinely read the tract's content; none read the branch point.

The 5 discovered tract-readers: content-destroying vs. shuffle-in-place attention drop

Δ attention mass (clean − corrupted/shuffled) from the masked-AG query into the tract, for 4 of the 5 tested heads that pass content-control (L0H14 omitted — real but ~1000x smaller in magnitude, since it's already ~100% saturated). L5H8 and L4H12 drop under both full corruption and composition-preserving shuffle — order-sensitive, matching the m_acc tract signal's own order-dependence.

content-destroying shuffle-in-place

All 6 candidates carried over from the donor-circuit proposal (L0H2, L1H14, L2H7, L2H0, L3H10, L1H1) spend 80–100% of the masked-AG query's attention on the tract's 15nt window — but that mass barely moves when the window's content is destroyed. That's the signature of a previous-token / local-window head, not a content reader: 0/6 pass content-control, at either region.

Ranking all 96 heads by actual clean-condition attention (free from the same forward passes) surfaced a completely different, non-overlapping set of top branch-point attenders. Testing those 15 discovered heads found 5 genuine tract readers (L5H8, L4H12, L3H5, L0H13, L0H14) — real, statistically robust drops under corruption, well below the saturated candidates' ~100% attention share (6–38% of budget). Zero of the 21 tested heads pass content-control for the branch point — some even attend more once it's corrupted.

Phase 5 — the intervention ladder: necessity, mediation, routing vs. content

results/phase5_results.json
5
Correlation vs. causation
Mean-ablation, restoration patching, and the Q/V split all come back null for L5H8/L4H12 individually. Even jointly ablating all 5 discovered heads barely clears significance, at ~2% of the real tract-corruption effect.

Mean-ablation (necessity): single heads vs. joint groups

Δ m_acc (clean − ablated) at each head's output, on the clean sequence. Filled = 95% CI excludes 0. L0H2 is a negative control — Phase 4's most attention-saturated donor-circuit head, which failed content-control.

individual head control head joint group

Zero of the five heads individually pass necessity — every CI straddles 0, including the control. Jointly ablating the four real readers together is still not significant. Jointly ablating all five barely clears 0 (−0.030 [−0.060, −0.002]) — but that's only ~2% of the size of the real PPT-corruption effect (+1.2 to +1.4), confirming these five heads' direct AG-position output, even summed, are nowhere near sufficient to explain the tract effect alone.

Restoration patching and the Q/V split are null too: patching a single head's true clean-run output back into a corrupted or shuffled sequence recovers roughly 0% of the clean-vs-degraded gap for both L5H8 and L4H12 — so there's nothing left to decompose into routing (Q-patch) vs. content (V-patch) components.

Read plainly: Phase 4's attention filter correctly separated genuine content-readers from positional-locality artifacts, but attention content-sensitivity does not imply single- or small-group causal necessity here. The tract-reading computation is either more distributed across components than these five, routed indirectly through positions this layer-local intervention can't see, or both — next-steps item 6 broadens the search rather than deepening it on this fixed list.
