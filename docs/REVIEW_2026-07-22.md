# Paper-review of `gfm-circuit-discovery`

**Reviewer pass — 2026-07-22.** Scope: the current scientific record (SpliceBERT donor circuit,
period-3 clock, ≈10.63 carrier, Head–Context Atlas), the methodology/evidence-ladder framing, the
figure set, and repository organization. Written as a peer reviewer would, with an eye to what is
publishable now, what needs work, and how to formalize the method.

---

## 0. Bottom line

This is an unusually disciplined project. The central move — refusing to let a striking observation
acquire causal or biological language without the experiment that licenses the promotion — is exactly
the discipline the genomics-FM interpretability literature is currently missing, where interpretation
still rests largely on attention-weight visualization and probing. The repository is closer to
publishable than the framing ("not publishable yet") suggests; the real gaps are (a) formalizing the
method in the field's established vocabulary, (b) one generalization experiment, and (c) a
consolidation pass on nomenclature and result-file organization. None of these is a science problem.

**Publishability verdict:** two publishable results plus a methods contribution exist *now*, scoped
honestly. See §3.

---

## 1. What needs explanation (a reader/reviewer will stumble here)

1. **"Circuit" vs. minimality — resolved internally, but front-load it.** The repo's strongest and most
   counter-intuitive claim is that the named circuit is *not* minimal (F≈0.005 when scrubbed; 72/103
   nodes for 90% faithfulness; no knee). This is scientifically the most interesting part (see §2) but a
   reviewer skimming will read "circuit" and expect a sparse graph. The definition is stated in README,
   MECHANISM, and CLAIMS — good — but the *headline* still says "circuit." Consider "structured causal
   core within a distributed computation" as the standing phrase everywhere the word first appears.

2. **The A0→A12 artifact namespace is opaque.** `a1`…`a12`, `ic1`, `preflight_*`, `a6_reconcile`,
   `a9c_sweep_seeded_draws` mean nothing without the DECISIONS_A0 map. The C-number claim ledger is the
   Rosetta stone, but the *file* names don't carry it. A reader landing in `results/splicebert_coupling/`
   cannot tell canonical from exploratory from superseded. (Fix in §4.)

3. **Two positional carriers with adjacent wavelengths (10.63 model vs 10.298 nucleosomal) is a subtle
   point that is easy to misread as "the model found nucleosome positioning."** The figures handle this
   well (fig4 panel C is excellent), but the prose in secondary docs should never state the two numbers
   without the "0.33 nt apart, model fit to ±0.005, powered refutation" qualifier in the same sentence.

4. **The relationship between the donor circuit (SpliceBERT.1024nt) and the ≈10.63 carrier
   (SpliceBERT.510nt multi-species) spans two checkpoints.** This is stated in fig captions but not
   prominently in MECHANISM. A reviewer will ask whether cross-checkpoint claims are being mixed. Add one
   explicit line: which checkpoint each result family lives on and why they are not conflated.

5. **"Payload" is named as a mechanism component before its semantics are known.** The repo is scrupulous
   that its *identity* is unresolved (R22), but the word "payload" implies content. This is fine if the
   first use always carries "acceptor-local, semantic identity unresolved."

## 2. What is especially interesting, and why

- **The no-knee faithfulness curve (C27/C33) is the most scientifically valuable finding, and it is
  under-sold.** The result that a necessary, sufficient-when-injected core is *99.5% non-faithful under
  scrubbing* because generation is distributed is a direct, empirical instance of the tension the
  interpretability field is actively arguing about: sufficiency ≠ faithfulness ≠ minimality. Most circuit
  papers report a sparse graph and a single faithfulness number; this repo reports the whole
  faithfulness-vs-size curve and shows there is no sparse explanation. That is a stronger and more honest
  epistemic object than a tidy circuit, and it maps *exactly* onto the MIB benchmark's CPR/CMD
  formulation (area under the faithfulness-vs-size curve). Naming it in that vocabulary would let the
  field immediately place the result. This is a headline, not a caveat.

- **The powered refutation of the nucleosome bridge (C14, 10.298 ≠ 10.63).** Reporting a *powered* null
  that kills your own most attractive biological story — with the sensitivity calibration to prove the
  assay could have found the effect — is exactly the kind of result that makes reviewers trust everything
  else in the paper. Keep it prominent.

- **The address/payload factorial (C23).** Showing that L3H1's contribution requires the positional
  attention address and the acceptor value payload *jointly* (near-pure interaction: address-only +0.01,
  payload-only +0.05, joint +0.37) is a clean mechanistic decomposition and a genuinely satisfying
  "how" — the head is doing a bind-and-convert operation, not a lookup.

- **The retraction ledger (R1–R24) as a first-class artifact.** Twenty-plus documented failure modes
  (no-op random controls, label-leaking forced choice, architecturally-forced K/V swaps, zero-hit
  permutation p-values, strand-dropping bugs) is, by itself, a teaching contribution to the subfield.

## 3. Publishable results and their evidence

### Result 1 — A causal donor–acceptor circuit in SpliceBERT (PUBLISHABLE NOW)

**Claim:** SpliceBERT's masked-donor prediction causally depends on its paired acceptor, implemented by a
traceable, information-specific pathway (acceptor read by L3H1 → MLP3/MLP4 assemble → low-rank `U_info`
→ L4/L5 transporters → MLM head), with a period-3 positional address and an acceptor-local payload bound
at L3H1.

**Why it clears the bar:** The prior published observation (SpliceBERT — Chen et al., bioRxiv 2023,
published *Briefings in Bioinformatics* 2024; verify the exact figure/section you attribute the
attention observation to) was *correlational* — elevated donor–acceptor attention. This work elevates
that to behavior-level causal
evidence (C1–C6, C16–C19, C23) with necessity, mediation, directed path, information specificity, and a
held-out replication of `U_info` across disjoint genes (C26). A full causal circuit trace in a genomic
foundation model is, to my knowledge, new; the field's GFM interpretability is dominated by attention
maps, probing, and clustering. This is the delta.

**Supporting evidence:** 52/52 headline quantities independently verified against controlling artifacts;
memorial test locks pass; sealed chr8/9 split; 13 preregistered gates.

**Residual risk (reviewer will press):** generality beyond canonical short introns and matched
clean/corrupt patching. See gap G1 in §5.

### Result 2 — Two positional carriers: one causal clock, one bounded-at-attention (PUBLISHABLE NOW)

**Claim:** A learned rank-2 period-3 code causally supports coding prediction and donor–acceptor routing
(deletion 0.946→0.027; C36 clean phase-aligned-use confirmation); a *distinct* ≈10.63 carrier is read by
8 heads post-softmax but its downstream use/origin are unknown; the tempting nucleosome bridge is
refuted with a powered assay.

**Why it clears the bar:** The honesty structure *is* the contribution — a causal positional mechanism, a
clearly-bounded one, and a refuted biological hypothesis, all on one evidence ladder. This is publishable
as-is, likely as the second half of the same paper or a companion.

### Result 3 (methods) — The evidence ladder as transferable practice (PUBLISHABLE WITH FORMALIZATION)

This is the longest-lived contribution and the one the user correctly flags as not yet publication-grade.
It is not a science gap; it is a formalization gap. See §6.

### The Head–Context Atlas

Currently best framed as *supporting* infrastructure and a source of the matched cohorts for the
generalization experiment (§5), not as a standalone headline. Its four validated general-use components
are solid; the context-specific signals correctly did not clear the frozen precision gate. Do not lead
with the Atlas — lead with Result 1 and use the Atlas as the substrate for the transduction test.

## 5. The generalization gap (the one experiment that most de-risks review)

**G1 — cross-example / cross-context transduction.** Almost all donor-graph causal evidence is
intra-example clean/corrupt patching on canonical short introns. Activation patching, by construction,
speaks only to the prompt distribution it is run on. A reviewer *will* ask whether the circuit generalizes
or whether patching merely works within matched pairs. MECHANISM.md and ROADMAP.md already specify the
decisive test: derive `U_info` (or a site-choice direction) on discovery genes, inject it into
gene-disjoint targets matched on site type/distance/strength/coding status/norm, and require a
dose-ordered target-margin change above matched-random and within-example controls. Running even a modest
version of this converts "a mechanism on the tested distribution" into "a transferable
information-bearing state" and removes the most likely rejection reason. The Atlas matched cohorts are
already the substrate.

**G2 — payload semantics (C30).** The paper can ship without resolving this *if* it is scoped out
explicitly (it currently is). If you want to close it, ROADMAP Track 2 (site-choice steering with paired
contrasts, not absolute-strength labels) is the right design. Do not let "splice-site strength" back in
without external validation.

If you would rather hold the paper until stronger: the "meantime" work that fills the gap is exactly
Tracks 1 and 2 in `docs/ROADMAP.md` — they are already designed. G1 is the higher-leverage of the two.

## 6. Making the methodology publication-grade — mechanistic-interpretability guidance

The evidence ladder is a genuine contribution, but it currently reads as a bespoke local framework. Ground
it in the field's established formal vocabulary so reviewers can place it and so the claims inherit the
credibility of the underlying theory. Concretely:

1. **Adopt the MIB faithfulness-curve formalism.** Your `a12_faithfulness_size` "no-knee, 72/103" result
   is close in spirit to the Mechanistic Interpretability Benchmark (MIB; Mueller et al., 2025,
   arXiv:2504.13151), whose circuit-localization track scores methods by two size-swept scalars: CPR
   (Circuit Performance Ratio) and CMD (Circuit–Model Distance/Difference), both computed across circuit
   sizes rather than at a single threshold. Report analogous size-swept scalars for your faithfulness
   curve. You independently arrived at the right kind of object (a curve, not a point); framing it in
   MIB's terms is low-cost credibility and lets others compare. Note MIB targets a per-task circuit;
   your contribution is orthogonal — you show there is *no* sparse knee — so cite it as the evaluation
   vocabulary, not as a method you are reproducing.

2. **Frame the "circuit" definition in causal-abstraction terms.** The causal-abstraction framework
   ("Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic Interpretability", Geiger et al.,
   arXiv:2301.04709, 2023; published JMLR vol. 26, 2025) unifies activation patching, path patching,
   causal scrubbing, and causal mediation under interchange interventions, and gives a precise notion of
   *graded* mechanistic faithfulness — close to what your evidence ladder measures column by column. Cite
   it as the formal backbone; define each ladder level as an interchange-intervention accuracy at
   increasing granularity. This is the single change that most directly answers "the formalization of our
   methods is not publishable yet." (I verified the framework and authorship against the arXiv/JMLR
   record; confirm the exact scope match against the paper before adopting its notation wholesale.)

3. **State the causal-scrubbing relationship explicitly.** Your C27 scrub-to-corrupt faithfulness test is
   causal-scrubbing-adjacent. Causal scrubbing is a Redwood Research method (Chan et al.,
   AI Alignment Forum, Dec 2022; not peer-reviewed — cite as such). Say where you follow it and where you deviate
   (e.g., you scrub to the corrupt distribution and report graded recovery rather than a binary
   hypothesis-consistency verdict). Reviewers reward this kind of explicit positioning.

4. **Cite patching best practices to pre-empt the hyperparameter critique.** Zhang & Nanda ("Towards Best
   Practices of Activation Patching in Language Models: Metrics and Methods", ICLR 2024) show localization
   results are sensitive to corruption method and metric; Heimersheim & Nanda ("How to use and interpret
   activation patching", arXiv:2404.15255, 2024) treat noising (necessity) and denoising (sufficiency) as
   non-symmetric. Your work already (a) uses in-distribution symmetric-token-style corruption (real
   non-partner/other-donor AG swaps, not Gaussian noise), (b) uses a continuous logit-margin metric, and
   (c) separates necessity from sufficiency. Say all three explicitly in Methods — you are already
   compliant; make it legible.

5. **Consider automated edge attribution (ACDC / EAP) as a method-agnostic cross-check.** The hand-built
   graph plus the held-out size curve is strong, but "which 72 nodes" derived from a static ranking is an
   upper bound (you say so). Running an automated circuit-discovery pass — ACDC (Conmy et al., "Towards
   Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability", NeurIPS 2023) or the cheaper Edge
   Attribution Patching (Syed, Rager & Conmy, 2023) — and showing it recovers the same structured core
   would make the faithfulness curve method-independent and blunt the "you found the circuit you went
   looking for" objection.

6. **The seven-stage workflow and the tool-to-question map (METHODOLOGY.md) are excellent and should be a
   figure**, not just prose — a schematic of the ladder with your SpliceBERT results overlaid as filled/
   open cells is the paper's methods hero image.

## 7. Bridging mechanistic interpretability to known genomic phenomena

This is the scientific heart of the ask. For each highlighted element, the honest bridge and the test:

**L3H1 sub-circuit ↔ splice-site pairing / intron definition.** The behavior — a donor scored by its
*specific* paired acceptor across the intron — is the model's implementation of cross-intron splice-site
communication. The short-intron scope (60–150 nt) places the result squarely in the **intron-definition**
regime (as opposed to exon definition), which is the biologically appropriate frame for short introns.
Strengthen the bridge by connecting partner-specificity and the distance dependence to the intron-
definition literature, and by testing whether the circuit's coupling strength tracks known determinants
(intron length limits, 5′SS/3′SS complementarity to U1/U2 recognition). This is the most defensible
biological bridge you have and it is currently under-stated.

**Period-3 carrier ↔ codon / reading-frame 3-periodicity.** The 3-nt periodicity of coding sequence
(non-uniform base composition across codon positions) is a textbook genomic signal — the basis of
Fourier-based ORF detection, gene-finders, and ribosome-profiling frame analysis. The natural hypothesis
is that the clock *originates* from codon-composition periodicity in the coding portion of the training
corpus; C36's phase-aligned use in CDS blocks is consistent. Crucially, you have already ruled out the
naive over-reach: `U_info` is phase-*independent* (flat across intron-length mod 3 and annotated splice
phase, C19), so the clock's present-day role is positional *addressing*, not reading-frame encoding. The
bridge to state cleanly: "candidate origin = codon 3-periodicity; present-day causal role = relative-
positional routing coordinate; origin unproven pending training-time evidence (checkpoints / corpus
ablation / cross-model)." Do not claim coding *enrichment* — the matched CDS-minus-non-CDS-exon contrast
was inconclusive (C36), and you correctly do not.

**≈10.63 carrier ↔ nucleosome / DNA helical periodicity — the bridge is REFUTED, and that is the result.**
The famous ~10.2–10.5 bp genomic periodicity (WW/SS dinucleotides, DNA helical repeat, nucleosome
positioning) is the obvious candidate. You measured the real nucleosomal signal (≈10.298 nt, z=+249) and
the model carrier (≈10.63 nt, fit to ±0.005) and showed they are *different* (0.33 nt apart) — a powered
refutation, not a null. The correct published statement is therefore: "the obvious biological bridge is
excluded; the ≈10.63 carrier's biological correlate is unknown and we make no claim." This is a strength.
The open scientific question — what *is* a 10.63-nt positional periodicity the model manufactured that is
neither codon nor nucleosomal — is a good hook for future work, not a hole.

**General principle for the paper:** a genomic FM circuit can have (a) a clear biological bridge (L3H1 →
splice-site pairing), (b) a candidate-but-unproven origin (period-3 → codon periodicity), and (c) a
refuted obvious bridge with an unknown true correlate (≈10.63). Presenting all three side by side, each
stopped at the correct evidence level, is a more credible paper than one that forces a biological story
onto every internal feature — which is precisely the failure mode of the current attention-visualization
literature.

## 8. Figures and visual-design recommendations

The existing figures are already publication-caliber — clean, honest, colour-threaded (period-3 blue /
≈10.63 orange), with the narrative in captions rather than the artwork. The
`make_fig_report_data.py` → validated data package → `make_figN.py` pipeline, with recompute-checks before
write, is exactly the right reproducibility discipline. Specific notes:

- **Keep** the fig1 "two carriers, two stories" layout and fig4 panel C (the wavelength zoom with the
  refutation). Those are the two most reviewer-persuasive panels.
- **fig5 / deck_circuit_graph** — the linear circuit schematic is clear. For the *directed graph* version,
  generate it from the edge JSON (`a10_generator_edges`, `donor_heads`) via graphviz/dot so topology is
  data-derived and reproducible, rather than hand-placed. This also future-proofs it against edge updates.
- **The 3D `carrier-wave-interaction-paper.svg`** is beautiful and good for a talk/cover, but 3D surfaces
  are hard for reviewers to read quantitatively. For the paper body, pair it with (or replace by) a 2D
  heatmap of the same attention-weight surface; keep the 3D as a conceptual/supplementary panel and label
  it as such.
- **Methods hero figure:** render the evidence ladder (P/QK/Attention/OV/Behavior/Biology) as a schematic
  with each result family's cells filled/open. This single figure communicates the whole disciplinary
  contribution.
- **Tooling:** the current matplotlib + hand-SVG stack is appropriate; no tool change needed. For the
  multi-panel composite, a grid-based composer (consistent panel letters, shared fonts/colour tokens)
  will save iteration. Standardize a single style module (fonts, palette, DPI, PDF+PNG) imported by every
  `make_figN.py` so the set is visually uniform on submission.
